2. Period and sub-genre

For each 25-year period, each composer with work pages in it gets their own sub-genre shares, and these are averaged (one vote per composer; n is the number of composers). This keeps prolific composers (Telemann 1,431 pages, Bach 1,246) from taking over a period. Numbers are % (5% and over only); hover for the pooled share over all work pages. The colour strip on the left is the big group. Periods with fewer than three composers (faded cells) are one or two people's shares.

3. Dating and duration

On IMSLP a single song and a whole symphony are one work page each, so counting pages inflates the share of vocal music and short pieces for composers who wrote many of them. Shares by duration use only pages that give a duration ('with duration'). Where the two shares differ much, the counting unit is changing the picture.

4. Method and limits

  • Big groups use the classification of the 1.2 'Works & history' page (IMSLP genre tags and instrumentation), with vocal split into sacred and secular. Sub-genres come from tag rules (SUB in genre_life_111.py); the last sub-genre of each group ('… etc.') holds works the earlier rules did not catch. 'Other', outside every group, is 0.8%.
  • Classification accuracy. An independent reviewer (Codex) reclassified work pages blind under the same scheme, and agreement is the share of pages where the reviewer's answer equals the rules'. The thresholds fixed beforehand were 95% for big groups and 85% for sub-genres; 1.12.1 added 85% for each of the three secular vocal sub-genres. There were seven rounds. Each drew a fresh sample that left out the pages of earlier rounds, and the rules were revised before the sample was drawn.

    1.11 (rounds 1 and 2). The first round led to fixes for five systematic faults (trio sonatas, overture-suites, songs with chorus, organ, sonatinas). In the second round the weakest sub-genre was 'Choral pieces, madrigals etc.' (64% of 14 pages): solo songs went to choral, and canticles with sacred texts went to secular.

    1.12.1 (rounds 3 and 4). Secular vocal music was divided anew. A piece for one voice is a song or an aria depending on its accompaniment; two or more voice parts go to 'Choral pieces, vocal ensembles, madrigals etc.'; cantatas, serenatas and odes go to 'Arias, secular cantatas' before any chorus marker is read. Sacred markers were added (canticles, Ave Maria and others), and 'bass' and 'tenor' naming instruments are no longer read as voices. In the third round 'Choral pieces, vocal ensembles, madrigals etc.' fell short at 80.4% of 56 pages. Following the disagreements turned up two faults in the big groups: stage works tagged other than 'operas' (lyric dramas, dramma giocoso and others; 34 pages, 13 of them Verdi operas) sat in secular vocal, and 51 pages with a vocal genre name but a keyboard-only scoring (songs without words, organ chorales) sat in vocal. The fourth round measured the classification with these two fixed; the third-round figures are from before the fix.

    1.12.2 (round 5). The faults seen in the fourth round's disagreements were fixed, on one principle: the scoring in the tags comes before the genre name. A keyboard-only scoring makes a keyboard work even when the name is concerto or overture (49 pages: Bach's concertos for solo keyboard, Telemann's keyboard overtures and others). Scoring codes with a number in front, such as 2vn bc, were not being read, which had left trio sonatas and the like in 'Other'; they are now chamber music ('Other' goes from 247 pages to 81). String orchestra and wind band go to orchestral, mechanical instruments (flute clocks, music boxes) to other, and suites without voices drawn from stage works (the Nutcracker suite and four more) to orchestral. For pages without tags, only 'Concerto' and 'Requiem' are read from the title. The fifth round measured this classification.

    1.12.3 (rounds 6 and 7). The faults seen in the fifth round's disagreements were fixed. A work scored for string quartet (two violins, viola, cello) is a string quartet whatever its name (22 pages). A work named concerto whose tags give nine parts or fewer (continuo counts as one part, however many play it) and no orchestra or string ensemble is chamber music (56 pages: Telemann's concertos in four parts, Vivaldi's chamber concertos and others). Pages with the Telemann catalogue number TWV 1 (church cantatas) are sacred cantatas whatever their tags (18 pages), and arias for one voice with continuo or keyboard are songs (9 pages). The 29 pages tagged 'secular oratorios' or 'secular hymns' had been in sacred vocal because of the words 'oratorios' and 'hymns'; they moved to secular vocal. In the sixth round 'Arias, secular cantatas' fell short at 84.1% of 44 pages. Five of the seven disagreements were secular oratorios, which the revision had sent to this sub-genre: the scheme had no place for them, and the reviewer's answers went different ways. So secular oratorios were written into the meaning of 'Arias, secular cantatas' (one line of the instructions), 14 pages with small faults seen in the sixth round were fixed (among them five Haydn notturni that sat in orchestral because a word in the instrumentation was misread), and a seventh round measured the result. The seventh round measures the present classification; the sixth-round figures are from before the fix. Of the 16 secular oratorios, however, 7 had already been used in rounds five and six, and only one fell into the seventh sample, so the seventh round says almost nothing about the effect of that change.

    What remains. The rules follow the IMSLP tags. In the fourth round, 5 of the 6 disagreements in 'Arias, secular cantatas' were works tagged 'secular cantatas' that the reviewer judged sacred from their texts and occasions (Bach and Mendelssohn). Of the 18 disagreements in the seventh round, 6 are works with secular tags (songs, arias, secular cantatas, carols and others) that the reviewer judged sacred from their texts, and 3 are arias written for insertion into operas (tagged 'arias') that the reviewer placed under stage. Tag rules cannot tell these apart. Brandenburg Concerto No. 6 is scored in its tags for six parts and continuo, which the rule would send to chamber music, but it is kept as a concerto: the Neue Bach-Ausgabe puts all six Brandenburg Concertos among the orchestral works (Series VII, vol. 2), and the German National Library's work record classes it as a concerto and a concerto grosso. Some writers describe it as string chamber music with one player to a part. Suites with chorus drawn from stage works (the Daphnis et Chloé suites and others) remain under stage. The scheme has no place of its own for wind band or mechanical instruments, which went to orchestral and other. From round three on, the instructions spelled out the borders of the three secular sub-genres more clearly than in round two (vocal ensembles and canons with choral; solo arias with orchestra with cantatas), and round seven added the line on secular oratorios, so the secular sub-genre figures are not like-for-like comparisons across rounds. With a few dozen pages per sub-genre the intervals are wide. All seven rounds are published as a CSV. Agreement is the share where the reviewer and the rules gave the same answer, not a rate of correct answers.

  • Work pages are not complete work lists: works without an IMSLP page, arrangements and pages titled for other composers are missing. Twentieth-century composers may have fewer pages for late works because of copyright, but how many works are missing, and why, cannot be checked from these data.
  • Works with no composition year but a first publication within the composer's life are placed at that year (hatched). A publication year only bounds the composition from above (whether it fell within the composer's life is checked by year only). Composers with more than 30% of works having neither a usable composition year nor a publication within their life (works with only a bound such as 'before 1720' included) are marked ⚠.
  • Colour: the six big groups use validated categorical colours, stacked in legend order so that touching colours stay apart under colour-vision-deficiency simulation. Sub-genres are lightness steps within the same hue. This borrows the aim of Tennekes & de Jonge (2014), showing a hierarchy through colour, but varies their method (which gives sibling categories different hues) by changing lightness only. Expanding a group shows only its steps and grey.
