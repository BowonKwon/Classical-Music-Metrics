Works & history

Ten composers' work activity over their lives, changes in symphonic orchestration, tempo markings and instrument use from IMSLP work pages, and the history of the pitch of a′ from Ellis's 19th-century tables. Added in 1.2.

1. Career ribbons2. Work pages by age3. When it was written, how much it is played4. Symphony orchestration5. Tempo markings6. Instrument use7. Pitch of a′

The unit is the IMSLP work page: one work can span several pages and one page can hold several works, so this is not a count of works composed. 4,589 pages for the ten composers (pages re-attributed to, or titled under the name of, another composer are dropped); of the 4,094 with a composition year, 247 that give only a bound ('before', 'after') are left out of year-based counts. Reception uses the NY Phil archive, matched by catalog number.

7. The pitch of a′, 1361 to the 1880s

At a glance

What the data show
Among 25-year medians (five or more points), the lowest is 418.8 Hz in 1750–1774 and the highest 453.0 Hz in 1625–1649. 235 records carry a date.
Watch for
The spread of the records Ellis collected, not each era's 'standard'; pitches of the same period differ by more than 100 Hz depending on place and use.

A. J. Ellis's table of historical pitches (Helmholtz, On the Sensations of Tone, tr. Ellis, 3rd ed. 1895, Appendix XX, Section H, Table I, pp. 495–504 — public domain), transcribed from the page images and checked line by line against the 2nd edition of 1885: 235 of 242 rows have a date. Each dot is one a′ record collected by Ellis. Most were measured from a fork, organ pipe or instrument, but some are computed from surviving pipes or from documents, and some are proposed or adopted standards (for example the Society of Arts' unexecuted standard of 1860 and the pitch adopted by the London Inventions and Music Exhibition of 1885). The horizontal axis is the date given in the table, which is not always the year of measurement: some organs carry the year they were built (the value for the Halberstadt organ of 1361 is Ellis's calculation from a model built to the pipe dimensions given by Praetorius), and periods such as 1740–1812 are plotted at their first year. Faint dots have dates given as before / after / about, or as a period. The black line is the median per 25-year bin (bins with five or more points); it weights records of different places, instruments and methods equally, and because it joins across sparse bins, a line passing through a year does not mean there are records for that year. Colors are a rough grouping by use, from words in the description. A maker's trade (organ-builder) is not treated as a use, and an opera, orchestra or band use takes precedence over the word 'organ' unless a church building is named. Some records belong to both, such as an organ in an opera house, so the colors are not suited to comparing uses across periods. Pitches of the same period differ by more than 100 Hz depending on place and use. This is the spread of the measured sample, not each era's 'standard'. The table ends in the 1880s.
