Scales and tuning

How the octave (1,200 cents) has been divided, compared across periods and regions. Each dot is a note; the grey ticks are piano equal temperament (100 cents apart). The buttons play the scale, synthesised by your browser on the spot.

Every scale is played on the same reference, middle C (261.63 Hz); the instruments themselves sit at other pitches. 'Against equal temperament' follows each note with the nearest equal-tempered note. The sound is a synthesised triangle wave, not the instrument's timbre. 'Theory' marks values set by ratios or rules; 'measured' marks values taken from instruments or performances.

1. Western tuning — values defined by ratios

Pythagorean tuning (major order)theory

Ancient Greece to medieval Europe · Pure fifths 3/2 folded into the octave (definition); cf. Scala archive pyth_12.scl

Archytas, enharmonic genus (E mode)theory

c. 4th century BCE, reported by Ptolemy · Tetrachord 28/27, 36/35, 5/4 (Ptolemy, Harmonics, book I); cf. Scala arch_enh.scl

Archytas, chromatic genus (E mode)theory

c. 4th century BCE, reported by Ptolemy · Tetrachord 28/27, 243/224, 32/27 (Ptolemy, Harmonics, book I); cf. Scala arch_chrom.scl

Archytas, diatonic genus (E mode)theory

c. 4th century BCE, reported by Ptolemy · Tetrachord 28/27, 8/7, 9/8 (Ptolemy, Harmonics, book I)

Just-intonation major scaletheory

Ptolemy's syntonic diatonic, arranged as a major scale by Zarlino (1558) · Ratios 9/8, 5/4, 4/3, 3/2, 5/3, 15/8 (definition); cf. Scala ptolemy.scl

Quarter-comma meantone (12 notes)theory

16th–17th-century Europe (Aron 1523) · Fifths narrowed by 1/4 syntonic comma, major thirds pure 5/4 (definition); cf. Scala meanquar.scl

Werckmeister III (12 notes)theory

Musicalische Temperatur, 1691 · C–G, G–D, D–A, B–F♯ narrowed by 1/4 Pythagorean comma, other fifths pure; cf. Scala werck3.scl

Vallotti (12 notes)theory

Mid-18th century; the book on tuning (book 2) was printed only in 1950, book 1 in 1779 · F–C–G–D–A–E–B narrowed by 1/6 Pythagorean comma, other fifths pure; cf. Scala vallotti.scl

12-tone equal temperamenttheory

The standard of the modern piano · Step 2^(1/12) = 100 cents (definition)

2. East Asia — sanfen sunyi (三分損益)

Sanfen sunyi (三分損益) shortens and lengthens a pipe by a third in turn, which amounts to stacking pure fifths. The twelfth note, 仲呂, lands at 521.5 cents rather than 4/3 (498 cents), because twelve fifths overshoot the octave by the Pythagorean comma (23.5 cents). The twelve yul of Korean court music follow the same series; in the ratio table of Yoo et al. (2011), jungnyeo (仲呂) is 1.35. The note sets of pyeongjo and gyemyeonjo follow convention, and whether performers actually placed jungnyeo near 521 cents cannot be told from these values.

Twelve lü by sanfen sunyi (三分損益)theory

Ancient China; Korean court music (aak) · Chain of pure fifths from 黃鍾; ratio table confirmed by Yoo et al. 2011 (J. Acoust. Soc. Korea 30(8), Table 4: jungnyeo 1.35)

Five tones gong–shang–jue–zhi–yu (宮商角徵羽)theory

Ancient China · First five notes of the fifths chain (definition)

Pyeongjo (黃 太 仲 林 南, sanfen sunyi values)theory

Korean court music · Note set of pyeongjo on hwangjong; 仲呂 at 521.5 cents by strict sanfen sunyi (eleven fifths above 黃鍾)

Gyemyeonjo (黃 夾 仲 林 無, sanfen sunyi values)theory

Korean court music, the older five-note gyemyeonjo (before it narrowed to three or four notes) · Note set of gyemyeonjo on hwangjong; strict sanfen sunyi values

The pitch of hwangjong changed over time

A scale is a set of ratios; the actual pitch is fixed by the reference note hwangjong (黃鍾). A study of 241 surviving stone-chime slabs (pyeongyeong and teukgyeong) in Korean collections grouped the stones by the cyclical year or the re-tuning date inscribed on them and estimated hwangjong from each group. A cyclical year recurs every 60 years, so the jeongyu and gapja groups do not fix a single year of manufacture.
