Piece map

Each movement on the site is treated as a vector of dozens of score metrics, and the vectors are laid out on a plane. Points close together are alike along these two directions. The two components hold only part of the spread (24% in the harmony map, 38% in the note map), so movements close on the plane can still differ on other metrics.

1. How it is made

  • For each map, each metric is standardized over the movements in that map (those with every value present; mean 0, population SD 1); principal component analysis finds the two directions of greatest spread, drawn as the horizontal (component 1) and vertical (component 2) axes.
  • The final computation uses three selection rules. The first two were fixed before computing: drop metrics whose |Spearman correlation| with log movement length exceeds 0.5, and those missing for more than 30% of movements (for the harmony map, counted among movements with harmony labels). The third, 'drop metrics defined only for some collections or pieces', was added after the first run dropped every string-quartet collection from the map.
  • Axes are not named. Instead the five metrics with the largest loadings are listed for each component. A component whose loadings change much when one collection is left out (correlation below 0.8) is marked unstable and not interpreted.
  • Left out — correlated with movement length: Pitch range, Pitch-class entropy, Top-line leap ratio, Top-line mean interval, Top-line stepwise ratio, Top-line range, Pitch spread, Pitch-name vocabulary (line of fifths), Duration vocabulary, Motif repetition, Chord vocabulary, Keys visited, Furthest key. defined only for some collections or pieces: Notes outside five octaves, Cadence density, Perfect authentic cadence share, Phrase length, 4- and 8-bar phrase share, Key-change distance.
