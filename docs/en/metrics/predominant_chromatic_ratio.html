Neapolitan and augmented-sixth ratio

At a glance

What the data show
Among the collections, Mozart's piano sonatas have the highest median (0.007) and Bach's English and French Suites and Bach's solo works (violin, cello, flute) the lowest (0.000). Within the piano sonatas: Beethoven 0.007, Mozart 0.007.
Watch for
Only for annotated corpora (not chorales). Inversions and applied uses of the Neapolitan are not separated.

Share of the two classic chromatic pre-dominant chords: the Neapolitan (♭II) and augmented sixths (German, Italian, French).

Formula
(chords with numeral bII + chords with chord_type Ger, It or Fr) / all chord labels
How to read it
Read together as the frequency of chromatic pre-dominant chords (the Neapolitan alone is absent from most movements, so every collection median would be 0). Mozart sonatas 0.007, Beethoven sonatas 0.007 and quartets 0.006, Bach suites and solo works 0 (no augmented sixths annotated). Among movements with harmony annotation, share using one at least once: Mozart 80% (43/54), Beethoven quartets 74% (52/70) and sonatas 73% (47/64), Bach solo works 31% (21/68) and suites 18% (16/89).
Caveats
Only for annotated corpora (not chorales). Inversions and applied uses of the Neapolitan are not separated. Beethoven's piano sonatas enter with the 64 of 91 movements that have harmony annotation.
