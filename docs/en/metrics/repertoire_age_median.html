Repertoire age

At a glance

What the data show
1842–43 15 → 2025–26 76 (completed seasons). The peak was 2021–22 (113; 79 concerts, 511 work performances).
Watch for
Uses the composer's death year, not the work's date. Composers without dates drop out, which may push recent values up.

How long the composers whose music is played have been dead — the median, weighted by work performances (new in 1.1).

Formula
per work performance (season start year − composer's death year), 0 for living composers; weighted median over performances with known dates (the first value at which the cumulative weight reaches half).
How to read it
15 years in 1842–43, 36 in 1922–23, 61 in 1962–63, 83 in 1982–83, 76 in 2025–26. The highest decade median is 96 years in the 2010s.
Caveats
Uses the composer's death year, not the work's date (work dates are largely missing in open data). Composers without dates drop out, which may push recent values up. Read with the living-composer share.

Source

New York Philharmonic Performance History: works, composers and conductors on more than 15,000 programs from the first concert in December 1842 to the current season. Besides the Philharmonic proper, the archive holds programs of the New York Symphony, the Stadium concerts and chamber series; this site does not filter them out. Updated nightly. https://github.com/nyphilarchive/PerformanceHistory · License: CC0 1.0

Wikidata (Wikimedia Foundation) person items: year of birth (P569) and death (P570). Composers with at least 10 NY Phil performances (U1) were searched by name; only human items with a music occupation were kept and checked against the composer's first season (born at least five years earlier). Names with several candidates or a failed check were resolved one by one by the editor. The matching table (composer_wikidata.csv) is published. https://www.wikidata.org/ · License: CC0 1.0
