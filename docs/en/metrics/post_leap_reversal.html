Post-leap reversal

At a glance

What the data show
Among the collections, Bach's English and French Suites have the highest median (0.719) and Bach's solo works (violin, cello, flute) the lowest (0.611). Within the piano sonatas: Beethoven 0.691, Mozart 0.676.
Watch for
Reversal after a leap can arise just from the line drifting back toward the middle of its range (regression to the mean; von Hippel & Huron 2000, Music Perception 18(1)), so a high value is not proof of a deliberate rule.

Share of top-line leaps (minor third or more) followed by a move in the opposite direction.

Formula
in the top-line interval sequence with repeated notes removed, intervals following one of ≥3 semitones that go the other way / intervals following one of ≥3 semitones Grace notes are left out, and a tied continuation is merged into the note it continues and counted once. A continuation whose preceding note cannot be found is not counted as a new note (it remains a sounding note only for the top-line metrics).
How to read it
Measures only the direction part of the rule that a leap is followed by motion the other way (whether the skipped notes are actually filled is not checked). Compare with reversal after steps (1–2 semitones): in all six collections reversal follows 0.61–0.72 of leaps but only 0.39–0.42 of steps.
Caveats
Reversal after a leap can arise just from the line drifting back toward the middle of its range (regression to the mean; von Hippel & Huron 2000, Music Perception 18(1)), so a high value is not proof of a deliberate rule. The top line is the chain of highest notes. When notes of the same pitch overlap, top-line extraction keeps the one that ends first, so a new onset at that pitch can be missed.
