Rhythmic contrast (nPVI)

At a glance

What the data show
Among the collections, Beethoven's string quartets have the highest median (29.5) and Bach's solo works (violin, cello, flute) the lowest (13.6). Within the piano sonatas: Beethoven 22.1, Mozart 23.4.
Watch for
Patel and Daniele used the written note values of themes; here the inter-onset intervals of the whole movement's top line are used (rests attach to the preceding note), so values are not directly comparable with that study.

How much successive top-line inter-onset intervals differ — a speech-rhythm index (Grabe & Low 2002) that Patel & Daniele (2003, Cognition 87(1)) applied to musical themes.

Formula
nPVI = 100/(m−1) × Σ |dₖ − dₖ₊₁| / ((dₖ + dₖ₊₁)/2), where dₖ are top-line inter-onset intervals (quarter notes) and m is their number Grace notes are left out, and a tied continuation is merged into the note it continues and counted once. A continuation whose preceding note cannot be found is not counted as a new note (it remains a sounding note only for the top-line metrics).
How to read it
0 if every interval is the same; higher as long and short alternate. Bach solo works 13.6 (even note values), Beethoven quartets 29.5, piano sonatas Beethoven 22.1 and Mozart 23.4.
Caveats
Patel and Daniele used the written note values of themes; here the inter-onset intervals of the whole movement's top line are used (rests attach to the preceding note), so values are not directly comparable with that study. With no French or English composers in the sample, the link to speech rhythm is not tested here. When notes of the same pitch overlap, top-line extraction keeps the one that ends first, so a new onset at that pitch can be missed.
