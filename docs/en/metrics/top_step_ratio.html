Top-line stepwise ratio

At a glance

What the data show
Among the collections, Bach's chorales have the highest median (0.706) and Beethoven's piano sonatas the lowest (0.407). Within the piano sonatas: Beethoven 0.407, Mozart 0.504.
Watch for
The 'top line' is the chain of highest notes, not a designated voice, so it can differ from the melody where accompaniment figures rise above it.

Share of top-line moves by a semitone or tone (1–2 semitones).

Formula
top-line intervals of 1–2 semitones / all top-line intervals. Repeated notes (0) count in the denominator, not the numerator. Grace notes are left out, and a tied continuation is merged into the note it continues and counted once. A continuation whose preceding note cannot be found is not counted as a new note (it remains a sounding note only for the top-line metrics).
How to read it
The counterpart of the leap ratio. Bach chorales have a median of 0.706 (four-part vocal lines), Beethoven piano sonatas 0.407. Within the piano sonatas Mozart's 0.504 is higher than Beethoven's.
Caveats
The 'top line' is the chain of highest notes, not a designated voice, so it can differ from the melody where accompaniment figures rise above it. When notes of the same pitch overlap, top-line extraction keeps the one that ends first, so a new onset at that pitch can be missed.
