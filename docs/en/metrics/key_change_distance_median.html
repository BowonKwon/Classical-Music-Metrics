Key-change distance

At a glance

What the data show
Among the collections, Bach's English and French Suites, Bach's solo works (violin, cello, flute), Beethoven's string quartets, Beethoven's piano sonatas and Mozart's piano sonatas have the highest median (1.0) and Bach's English and French Suites, Bach's solo works (violin, cello, flute), Beethoven's string quartets, Beethoven's piano sonatas and Mozart's piano sonatas the lowest (1.0). Within the piano sonatas: Beethoven 1.0, Mozart 1.0.
Watch for
A move to the relative key (C major ↔ A minor) is 0, to the parallel key (C major ↔ C minor) 3.

Median number of steps the key signature moves when the local key changes (new in 1.9).

Formula
Take each local key's signature (sharps +, flats −; a minor key takes its relative major's signature), and at each change count the difference, folded enharmonically to 0–6. Median over the movement; blank if the key never changes. Local-key numerals are read as in the DCML standard: in a minor context III, VI and VII are the degrees of the natural minor (III in C minor is E♭ major); a nested label such as vi/V is vi of the key of V, each part read in the mode of the key to its right.
How to read it
1 means a move to a neighboring key on the circle of fifths (C major ↔ G major, for example). In 80% of the movements that change key the value is 1 or less, and in 9% it is 0. Most collection medians are 1; Dvořák's Silhouettes (2) and Liszt's Années de pèlerinage (3) are higher.
Caveats
A move to the relative key (C major ↔ A minor) is 0, to the parallel key (C major ↔ C minor) 3. Local keys are the annotators'. How often the key changes is measured separately by the modulation rate.
