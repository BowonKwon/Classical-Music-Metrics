Method & sources

Principles

  • Every metric carries its definition, formula, sample and caveats. That is what makes baseball's sabermetrics persuasive.
  • Measurement is kept apart from interpretation. "The chromatic ratio is higher" is a measurement; "so it is more innovative" is a reading. This site does only the former.
  • One codebase measures both composers. Measuring Bach and Beethoven differently would not be a comparison.
  • Only open corpora. Scores, MIDI and audio are not redistributed; only the numbers are.

Source

dcml — DCML corpora (Digital and Cognitive Musicology Lab, EPFL): Beethoven string quartets (ABC; Neuwirth, Harasim, Moss & Rohrmeier 2018), Beethoven piano sonatas (Hentschel et al. 2024), Bach English and French Suites and solo works (Hentschel et al. 2025), Bach chorales (converted from Kaiser 2002). Note, measure and harmony tables extracted from MuseScore files with ms3. https://github.com/DCMLab · License: CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 (chorales: CC0 1.0)

kern370 — Craig Sapp, Bach 370 chorales (Humdrum **kern, Breitkopf & Härtel numbering). Second, independent encoding used to cross-check the chorale metrics. https://github.com/craigsapp/bach-370-chorales · License: CC BY-NC-SA 4.0

bmetr — Martín-Castro & Ucar (2020), "Conductors' tempo choices shed light over Beethoven's metronome", PLOS ONE 15(12): e0243616. Track lengths and coda tempo estimates for 36 conductors' complete Beethoven symphony cycles (1,332 tracks), plus metronome marks and bar counts from the scores. R package bmetr. https://github.com/Enchufa2/bmetr · License: MIT

asap — ASAP dataset (Foscarin et al., ISMIR 2020). Piano MIDI performances aligned beat-by-beat to scores: 169 Bach (Well-Tempered Clavier) and 271 Beethoven (sonatas); 169 and 259 of these have beat counts matching the score and only those are used. Performances come from MAESTRO (International Piano-e-Competition). https://github.com/fosfrancesco/asap-dataset · License: CC BY-NC-SA 4.0

nyphil — New York Philharmonic Performance History: works, composers and conductors on more than 15,000 programs from the first concert in December 1842 to the current season. Besides the Philharmonic proper, the archive holds programs of the New York Symphony, the Stadium concerts and chamber series; this site does not filter them out. Updated nightly. https://github.com/nyphilarchive/PerformanceHistory · License: CC0 1.0

Comparison with the source paper

Martín-Castro & Ucar (2020) report, from a mixed-effects regression, that HIP, HIP-influenced and Romantic conductors fall below the marks by 6(2), 8(2) and 13(2) bpm. The plain medians here are -6, -8 and -12.7 bpm — the same picture by a simpler route.

Not yet here (long-term)

  • Score metrics for the Beethoven symphonies — no open machine-readable full scores; needs OMR plus human correction.
  • Bach recording durations over time (before/after the period-instrument movement) — requires de-duplicating reissues in release metadata.
  • Conductor tempo statistics beyond Beethoven.
  • Repertoire of orchestras other than the New York Philharmonic — bulk open data is rare.
